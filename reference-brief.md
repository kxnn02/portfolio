# Reference brief

What the design is borrowed from, and what it deliberately isn't.

Shortlist captured 20 Oct 2026 from awwwards (live sites, full-page, 1440px).
Screenshots and side-by-side comparison: `C:\Users\Admin\portfolio-shots\references.html`.

## The three borrowed decisions

### 1. Structure — one row per project, one big visual each

**From:** NexStudio (`https://nexstudio.tech`)

Each project is a single row on a hairline: meta line, name, one paragraph,
stack, links — and in the second column, one large visual, framed, on its own
ground. No card grid, no thumbnail mosaic. The row's height is set by the
visual, so the text column gets deliberate empty space underneath it.

**Why it fits:** the argument is "ships real software". A big picture of the
running thing makes that argument without a sentence. A grid of four small
cards makes it four times, at a quarter of the size.

**What was not borrowed:** NexStudio's floating 3D object and its flipped dark
work band. The 3D is the page's whole personality and it isn't Kenneth's work;
a dark band would fight the light-only decision.

### 2. Type — a display serif that interrupts the sans, and mono for every label

**From:** Signal-A Studio (`https://signal-a.studio`)

The opening statement mixes a heavy grotesque with an italic serif inside one
sentence. Metadata — years, stacks, indexes, captions — is entirely monospace
and small, while reading text stays in one sans at one size. Signal-A uses the
trick across a whole paragraph; here it's spent in two places only: the hero
("that *ships*.") and one clause in the About lede.

**Why it fits:** it gives the page a signature that costs one font file and no
JavaScript, and the mono/grotesque split is already how the content is shaped
(labels vs sentences).

**What was not borrowed:** the text-only project index and the awards table. A
text index hides the software; an awards table built from one internship and
four student projects would look thin.

### 3. Space — an uneven rhythm, and labels that whisper

**From:** AAT Architecten (`https://www.aat-architecten.be`)

Very little on the page is heavy. Rows are separated by generous vertical
space rather than boxes; captions and labels sit at 11–12px in a muted colour;
the two-column sections (About, Education & leadership) are deliberately
unequal, with the lighter column left short rather than padded out.

**Why it fits:** it keeps a four-project page from reading as a wall, and it
lets the one crimson accent do all the emphasis work on its own.

**What was not borrowed:** AAT's near-stillness and its reliance on
photography. UI screenshots need captions and context to mean anything, and the
owner asked for expressive motion.

## The accent

Not borrowed from any reference. Sampled from the owner's own product: the
CampusCurrents landing page's crimson is **#AE0F19** (the "Try the Beta" button
and the "Stay Safe." headline). It is used, intentionally, about six times on
the whole page — the italic in the hero, section numbers, link hovers, the
focus ring, the copy-email confirmation, and the Copy button's active state.

One colour sits outside that rule: the green availability dot, which is
semantic (status), not brand.

## Rejected after live checks

Four shortlisted awwwards entries did not survive a full-page capture:

- **Francisca Moura Portfolio 2022** — domain no longer resolves.
- **imotion factory films** — expired TLS certificate.
- **Huy Phan Vol.2** (Site of the Day, Developer Award) — the award screenshot
  shows a full portfolio; the live site is now a red wordmark splash.
- **Mario Carrillo** (Site of the Day, Developer Award) — a single full-screen
  WebGL shader wall. Beautiful, and it says nothing about what he builds.

All four still score highly on awwwards. The lesson is in the brief's spirit:
award thumbnails are not evidence — load the site.
